# Choose paths that add evidence

Use this when planning coverage or repairing a shallow result. A strand answers a consequential unknown or tests a competing explanation. It may draw on several kinds of source. A source community can also be a useful strand when it offers distinct access and parallel work helps. Choose the number from the work; one focused path can be enough.

When the field is unfamiliar, first learn enough vocabulary and landmark work to ask useful questions. Then revise the paths. Known authors, papers, products and communities are starting leads, not a required name density or a boundary around the inquiry. Follow what those sources reveal, including corrections to the leads themselves.

## Openings to consider

| Unresolved question | Places that may supply a different observation |
| --- | --- |
| What actually happens in the relevant setting? | Current artifacts, direct observations, measurements, operating records and firsthand accounts. |
| Why did people choose, abandon or change this approach? | Maintainer discussions, rejected attempts, release history, postmortems, practitioner accounts and records of the actual change. |
| What mechanism explains the pattern, and what would challenge it? | Original studies, replications, critiques, competing explanations and observations under different conditions. |
| What is stated or required, and how is it applied? | Current rules, documentation, contracts or specifications, paired with accounts of implementation and outcomes. |
| What limits the choice here? | Version and compatibility evidence, workload measurements, cost and availability records, maintenance experience, or local capability. |
| What has a different field solved about the same structure? | Work sharing a mechanism, constraint or human task. Preserve what transfers and what depends on a different objective or setting. |

These are openings, not required categories. Technology selection may split by compatibility, workload and operating cost. A policy question may split by the rule in force and observed application. An academic question may follow competing explanations. Keep the user's actual objective visible as paths change.

Check whether apparently separate paths repeat the same underlying study, announcement or incident. Independent observations add corroboration; more retellings do not. Seek relevant languages, less visible failures and sources with a different reason to report what happened.

## Repair a weak return

Find what prevented useful evidence. A list of links needs the decisive passages; repeated famous examples need new terms or source communities; conflicting conclusions need their populations, dates and conditions aligned. Sparse public records may call for a direct observation rather than more searches. Send a targeted follow-up to the existing collector when it can repair the gap, following the handoff and tool contracts.

Keep worthwhile surprises even when their application is unclear, but park them briefly instead of letting them displace the unresolved question. Close a path that can no longer change the answer within the available effort.
