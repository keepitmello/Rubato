# Preserve the evidence needed for judgment

Use this when several returns need synthesis or another reader must judge the material. Organize around the questions that change the answer, not a fixed outline. Keep a compact account of the decisive findings first; leave supporting records reachable rather than repeating them everywhere.

For each consequential finding, preserve the claim, its supporting source or passage, what was observed, the conditions in which it holds, and the contrary material that changes its meaning. Explain what follows for this inquiry. Use natural prose or a compact table as appropriate. Retain original figures and quotations where they carry the distinction.

Do not let shortening strengthen a claim. If selective removal helped but random removal hurt, the short summary must preserve that difference. If a result concerns an older web implementation, retain that scope when discussing a current mobile product. A missing published rationale stays a gap, rather than becoming proof of an arbitrary choice.

Deduplicate common origins and reconcile conflicts where different settings explain them. Preserve unresolved conflicts. Distinguish an underlying cause, evidence that something occurs, its prevalence and its effect on this user when those are different questions. Keep good indirect findings separately reachable without implying that they already decide the case.

State what was read and what the owner rechecked for the decisive claims. Link the original record, passage or saved material so the next reader can inspect it. Preserve access limitations inherited from collectors. Check the findings that the recommendation will depend on, rather than a fixed number of sources; this may require correcting scope or interpretation as well as a quotation or number.

For a fresh judge, include the user's purpose, binding constraints, relevant original words, current observations and the conditions of earlier attempts. Leave the sender's preferred verdict out of this evidence handoff. Evidence selection and weighing still require interpretation. Give access to material that challenges the summary, not just material selected to tell one story.

Shared material shares collection gaps even when readers judge independently. Keep source material reachable so a reader can challenge the digest itself. When requesting critique of an existing answer, invite agreement and material correction; forced opposition adds no evidence.

When this is the user's final deliverable, answer the requested question. Understanding needs a supported explanation; a choice needs a recommendation and the specific uncertainty that could change it. Do not stop at a source catalog. Note whether the remaining uncertainty is immaterial, needs a different observation, or remains because time or access ran out.
