# Deep-dive

Investigate to the user's requested endpoint: an evidence handoff, a recommendation, or an explicitly staged checkpoint. Judge relevance and meaning throughout. Give a final recommendation when the request includes one; when the request names no endpoint, hand off the evidence and leave the choice to the user. A research assignment already authorizes the investigation. Keep its stages visible as progress reports; a stage is a stop only when the user asked to stop there.

## Follow the unsettled question

Put the user's words, actual material and unresolved decision together. Preserve fixed facts, explicit limits, and rejected options with their reasons. Current explanations and candidates remain hypotheses; keeping the current approach is a valid outcome. New evidence may challenge an earlier rationale, but does not silently reopen a settled user constraint.

Ask what the decision requires before searching for solutions. Inspect supplied artifacts when they can change that question. Translate the underlying problem into terms used in other fields; treat known authors and projects as leads, not boundaries. Keep strong indirect material whose use is still unclear when it could change the framing.

One research owner—either the lead or the agent entrusted with the whole investigation—keeps questions, original evidence and provisional interpretations together. Let findings change the next search, reading depth or division of work. Parallelize questions that can advance independently, within the accepted budget. Before a favored explanation closes a consequential alternative, look for evidence that could distinguish them; revise the question when the alternative explains more.

## Read what the claim needs, and preserve its meaning

Open sources before reporting them as evidence; unopened leads remain leads. Prefer originals. Choose public or internal material according to where the answer can be observed. The owner examines the original material behind decisive or disputed claims, including context-preserving extracts returned by an authorized browser agent. A collector's summary alone is not that check.

Read far enough to test the claim and its limits: for a study, its method, sample, comparison and results; for a product claim, the platform, version and how behavior was established. Skim for discovery, then deepen where interpretation depends on it. Record the location and actual extent of inspection. A few frames do not establish motion at normal speed. Distinguish what the source claims, what was directly observed, and what you infer for this task. Assess provenance and incentives; several retellings of one source are not independent evidence.

Keep each decisive claim with the conditions, counter-results and scope that change its meaning through every summary and handoff. If brevity requires a cut, drop a less important claim rather than its decisive qualifier. Check the outgoing wording against the original: one person's reason does not describe an entire field, and missing evidence does not prove absence.

## Delegate with the question and the evidence intact

Give each research delegate its question, relevant user wording, facts, rejected options and reasons, and whether it owns collection or the whole investigation. Name the record file for the research owner; collectors return material for the owner to record. Ask delegates to read this skill when accessible; otherwise paste its reading and preservation rules. Supply the relevant text of any task material they cannot open. For a judgment consultation such as Outpost, follow its packet rules while preserving the question and decisive evidence conditions.

Return findings with their decisive conditions, source locations, inspection status, conflicts and remaining questions. For restricted sources, return the relevant original passage and surrounding conditions within existing access permissions. The receiving owner follows up on consequential gaps, normally through the same research delegate while the question remains within its remit; it need not repeat reliable checks merely because work was delegated. Aside and Outpost keep their own skills for execution.

## Finish where the requested work is complete

Continue, within the accepted budget, while a credible, accessible next check could materially change the requested result. Do not stop merely because one explanation sounds coherent or a collection phase ended. When a necessary experiment, observation or user judgment falls outside the requested stage or cannot be obtained here, state what would settle it. Mark proposed checks as unperformed; distinguish an access, time or budget limit from sufficient evidence.

Before handing off, compare the result with the user's limits and rejected choices. Check the decisive claims in drafted headings, summaries and recommendations against their supporting passages or calculations, including the assumptions needed to apply them here. Carry corrections through the current record and outgoing text. State what the evidence supports, what conflicts, what remains open, which material choices still depend on the user's priorities, taste or experience, and why the investigation stops here. Keep the research record in a file the next owner can open, and point to it from the working note. Rewrite changed judgments in place; log consequential search pivots, exclusions and superseded judgments with their reasons, and keep source records beside it. Mark missing history rather than reconstructing it as fact.
