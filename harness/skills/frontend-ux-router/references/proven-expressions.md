# Proven expressions

People arrive carrying habits from products they already use, so an expression
that already answered the same moment elsewhere is a good place to start
looking. Familiarity is a reason to consider a candidate; whether it fits here
is decided by its conditions and by what you observe.

## Use

Name the question the person has at that moment, in their words: "did it
save?", "can I undo this?", "is this pin one photo or many?". Check the
project's table first; an earlier answer and the reason it was chosen are the
cheapest evidence you have. The question is the key, not the component: "where
am I in this flow?" can be answered by a stepper, a progress bar or a shrinking
list, and the right one depends on the scene. Compare alternatives when they
differ in what the person understands or does.

Identify the relationship that answers the question and the conditions it
depends on. Check which differences in task, audience, controls or consequence
change that relationship here. A relationship can carry across fields; a
surface that looks close can fail to carry.

If the table leaves a consequential choice unresolved, investigate the missing
difference. Use a quick lookup when it settles the question, and a deeper
research round when conflicting or missing evidence needs one.

## The table

One table per project, next to its design documents:

| Person's question | Proven expression | Where | Our use | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|

"Source" records what was observed in the original product and what outcome, if
any, was reported. "Our use" records the proposed adaptation and any result
observed here. Keep recalled examples marked as recalled; verify the particular
claim when the design choice depends on it.

## Examples

A Roblox game project started the table (2026-10-25), with its user's words
「기존에 검증된 … 표현방법. 이런걸 앞으로 다가오는 문제마다 잘 조합해서
매칭한다면 작업효율이 좋아질거같아」. In the same run, the research suggested
that heavy players could benefit from a route, from "a wide uphill favours heavy
vehicles". The actual movement rules ruled out copying it: carrying ore reduced
walking and jumping. Resistance to crosswind offered a different relationship
to test. Calculation suggested an advantage, and the built course was then
measured because the fastest load changed with the settings. The question and
mechanism transferred; the route and numbers were worked out locally.

For services, research for a photo-map app found two open-source photo apps that,
after years of user requests, changed a cluster tap from zooming in to opening
the photos. That is a candidate to examine for photo browsing, not a decision
for the current screen.
